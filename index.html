bio

Joel Cook is an AI architect at Indian River State College in Florida, building in-house the software a public college would usually rent. Joel’s AI work is built for private data: answers grounded in official sources, read-only access to systems of record, and personal information kept out of the models. Before IRSC, Joel built HIPAA-compliant patient software on Epic for Ballad Health.

projects

RiverGuide

9,000+ chats a week · $47,000 a year less · 48% more accurate search

The college’s AI advisor. It answers from the college’s 2,400 official pages and links every answer to its source.

  • Search: a model we trained in-house, 48% more accurate, 26% faster, and 29% cheaper than keyword search, which stays as the fallback.
  • Privacy: Rampart Edge blocks names and ID numbers on the student’s device, and nothing students type is logged.
  • Links: every one is checked against the college’s pages before a student sees it.
  • Testing: every prompt, tool, or model change is graded on a 54-question test.
  • Model: one setting. A 27B Qwen on our own RTX 4090, Grok, or Claude.

Version one carries the 9,000+ chats a week on Microsoft Copilot Studio, with a source-cited wiki for explanations and a database for dates and dollar amounts.

pet projects

RiverGuide Retriever is Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B fine-tuned to find the college page a student means by RN, BSN, or rad tech. On 942 pages it never trained on, the right page is in the top 5 for 902 of them, against 631 for the BM25 index RiverGuide runs. Abbreviations it had never seen: 67 of 75, against 51. On 523 real student questions it found an answer for 88%, against 59%. It went live in RiverGuide on 24 October, where it searches first and Qwen answers with a third fewer model calls.
