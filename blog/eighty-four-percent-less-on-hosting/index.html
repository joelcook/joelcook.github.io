84% off the hosting bill, and it’s still too much

$50k became $8k for a site serving 250k visitors a month. Most people would take the win. I wanted to go further!

12 Sep, 2026

We were paying $50,000 to host a website with 250,000 monthly visitors. I got that down to $8,000. That’s 84% off! It’s the kind of number that looks fantastic on a resume, and I’m not going to pretend I didn’t put it on mine.

But think about that remaining $8,000 for a second. For serving a website! My honest reaction wasn’t triumph. It was: that’s still way too much.

So I wanted to go all the way and bring the whole thing on premises. That didn’t get approved. Not yet, anyway. Which means this story has a smaller ending than the one I wanted: a massive cut to the hosting bill, with the next move still sitting on paper.

I think that part is worth writing about too. Working inside an institution means plenty of decisions aren’t yours to make alone. You can see exactly where you want to go and still have to build inside the setup that got approved. That’s the job! And the cut we made counts, even if I wanted more.

Now, I’m not going to pretend on-prem is free. It isn’t. Backups, monitoring, updates, connectivity, and recovery all have to land on somebody’s desk. What I’m after is a lower total cost, including the work it takes to keep the thing running. Shuffling an expense off one invoice and onto somebody’s to-do list is just accounting theater.

But that’s exactly why you do the math instead of shrugging at the bill. Until the on-prem plan actually runs, it’s a pile of estimates, and it still has to prove itself. Fine! The $42,000 we already cut is real, and it’s a pretty sweet foundation to build on.

So here’s my question for you: when did you last take a hard look at what you’re paying to host your stuff? Because I bet the answer is more shocking than you think. For us, it’s $50k down to $8k so far. I’m happy with that. I’ll be happier with the next one.
