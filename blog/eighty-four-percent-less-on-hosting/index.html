84% cheaper, and I still want the bill lower

$50k became $8k for a site serving 250k monthly visitors. I wanted to go further.

12 Sep, 2026

I cut hosting costs from $50,000 to $8,000 for a website serving 250,000 monthly visitors. That is an 84% reduction, which makes for a satisfying line on a resume.

My reaction to the remaining $8,000 was that it still seemed expensive.

I wanted to take the site entirely on premises. We never got approval. So this story has a smaller ending than the one I wanted: a substantial reduction in the hosting bill, with the next move still on paper.

I think that is worth writing about too. Engineering inside an institution includes decisions you don’t get to make by yourself. You can see a direction you want to explore and still have to work within the setup that is approved. The reduction we achieved counts even though I wanted more.

The on-prem idea would need its own accounting. Backups, monitoring, updates, connectivity, and recovery still have to be somebody’s responsibility. I want a lower total cost, including the work required to keep the site running. Moving an expense off one invoice wouldn’t be enough.

The on-prem proposal still has to prove itself. Until it runs, its costs and maintenance demands are estimates. The reduction we already made gives me a real result to build on.

For now, the result is $50k down to $8k. I’m happy to have made that change. I’d be happier still to make the next one.
